### Recovery measurements, decisions and continuing service

Provider job identities remain scoped to their workload and repository. An identical source row can be received again without creating another job; a conflicting identity requires review. Corrections append the complete replacement, reason, correcting source and expected earlier version hash. Both the original observation and every later version remain available.

A recovery record identifies the exact plan, workload, repository and selected backup version where applicable. It retains recovery point, incident or exercise reference time, start, service-ready time, actual method, status and verification. The application calculates RPO from the recovery point to the reference time, RTO from the reference time to usable service, and execution duration from start to usable service. These are separate measurements, compared with the selected plan's targets.

The reviewer accepts or rejects the exact recovery version. Later corrections do not silently inherit that acceptance. Readiness brings together the approved plan, local and offsite evidence for its workloads, required recovery methods and the five operating criteria: monitoring, reporting, administrative access, training and deficiency closeout. Missing or unsuccessful evidence stays visible. A readiness result supports the customer's final review; it does not supply the customer's contractual sign-off.

Incident review retains discovery, impact, expected duration, restoration and actual communication observations. Email, telephone, continuing updates and the written post-incident report remain recorded operator actions; the software sends no notification and invokes no backup provider.

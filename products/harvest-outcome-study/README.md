# Harvest and Outcome Study Desk

A complete offline source study for physical harvests, supplied predictions, quality observations, reviewed allocations, actual dispatch and commercial outcomes.

## Open the application

Run `node native/harvest-desk.cjs build-html --out Harvest_Outcome_Study_Desk.html` from the supplied source directory. Open that self-contained file in a current browser. It has no network dependency. The complete authored workflow is available from Study scope; New study opens an empty operational study.

The ordinary interface provides all 28 CSV templates and explicit column mapping; original/returned edition selection; typed record correction with retained predecessors; source-backed grower, site, crop, logger, person, buyer, protocol and invoice context; scoped authority; exact forecast/model/plan bindings; physical and outcome views; source-bound human review; original binary documents; full session and ZIP exports; persistent browser saving and recovery of unfinished work and competing branches.

## What the complete course shows

Four original harvests become six physical batches. The original 260 kg reconciles to 150 kg actually dispatched,7 kg warehouse dispositions and103 kg retained. Derived lots preserve their original harvest families. Four supplied forecasts retain three observed censoring endpoints and one unobserved endpoint; the distance to the observed censoring intervals is an absolute-error lower bound. A lower forecast estimate fails one buyer requirement while its central estimate fits.

Three allocations retain actual authored operator review. Two deliveries have complete terminal outcomes; the third retains10 kg with an unknown outcome. Observed sales USD 320 and posted credits USD 20 are separate. Every created batch has all eight recurring-work phases on both methods, with 288 → 210 active person-minutes and increased forecast-review work.

All course people, authorities, model outputs, source documents and observations are explicitly authored. Original source text and the complete reconstructed study are retained.

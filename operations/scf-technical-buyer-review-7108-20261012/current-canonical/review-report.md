## Defined architecture and operating behavior

The architecture has six components: a private merchant source/release workspace; its public HTTP or MCP operation; the Stellar SDK and facilitator; private buyer approval and spending state; retained delivery records and complete output; and merchant fulfillment/accounting.

**Select and publish.** The merchant chooses an authorized source edition or named live query, schema, output and limits. Review resolves the route, network, asset, recipient, amount and authorization lifetime. Later source or terms changes become separately reviewed states.

**Connect the source.** Adapters preserve types, null values, pagination, keys, units, time basis and snapshot/live behavior. Customer-controlled credential or OAuth references select access. The returned result identifies its source edition.

**Approve and pay.** The buyer retains the HTTP method or MCP tool, arguments, scope and payment option. The remote schema and full challenge, including authorization lifetime, must match before signing. For paid MCP, the endpoint, tool and arguments remain separate from the payment resource; the official MCP SDK carries payment and settlement metadata. Per-payment limits and the existing reservation/budget owner govern aggregate spending.

**Settle and receive.** The facilitator verifies and settles the native authorization. Independent RPC reconciliation checks the network, asset, parties and atomic amount. The application retains the complete output, merchant notification and receiving acknowledgment. A multi-request acquisition keeps its aggregate cost and unfinished coverage. Saved prepaid calls retain their selected pass; a replacement links to its predecessor and acquired results remain available.

**Use and account.** The receiving application opens every page and file needed for the task. Merchant pricing connects the selected tariff and saved uniform quote to an actual usage period, counting basis and supplier-cost basis. A reviewed price change carries its measured source into the customer quotation and original invoice or service records.

**Recover and hand over.** Server confirmation, observed transfer, output and fulfillment retain separate states. An uncertain signed response is reconciled through its original dispatch. The receiving engineer restores configuration, history and customer files, supplies authorized secret references and continues the same operation.

Node.js supplies the merchant, buyer, browser, CLI, typed-client and MCP interfaces. Source adapters and receiving applications retain their native semantics. The maintained implementation also uses Python and SQLite for typed data and saved queries, and PHP for WordPress/WooCommerce integration. New contracts are outside this six-package request; the product uses existing SDKs and token contracts.

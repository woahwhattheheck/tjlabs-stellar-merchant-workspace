## Full application: replacement answer 3

### Why Stellar and what we contribute

Stellar gives this product a shared authorization and settlement layer. In the existing x402 Exact flow, a buyer signs authorization for a particular payer, recipient, token amount and expiry, and a facilitator verifies and submits it on the selected network. Explicit fee sponsorship can cover submission costs. We use existing Stellar SDKs and token contracts. Our Open Track proposal is an application-level improvement: keeping a complete customer task usable from the reviewed request and spending decision through delivery, recovery and independent maintenance. The requested track remains subject to the issuer's invitation and review.

The buyer's useful unit of work can be larger than one paid response. An analyst may need every page in a selected dataset and an editable report; a service operator may need recurring results, their original source context and a handover another engineer can resume. A transport receipt alone does not specify whether that agreed task is complete. Our implementation retains the exact HTTP request or MCP tool and arguments, selected source edition, payment terms, aggregate approval, original operations and all agreed outputs together. Buyers retain signing and spending authority; merchants retain source-access, pricing and fulfillment authority.

| Existing foundation | Documented technical and business role | Our application-level contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stellar x402 libraries and facilitators | Standard payment challenges, signed authorization, verification and settlement let merchants charge for resources using existing Stellar infrastructure. | Retain the concrete request, selected terms and total task approval with the original transfer and complete customer result, including tasks spanning multiple separately paid responses. |
| Stellar MPP SDK | Charge uses an on-chain transfer for each payment. Channel uses a funded Soroban channel and off-chain cumulative commitments, with later settlement. These are upstream payment capabilities. | The maintained MPP workspace carries selected service/task authority, original purchases and responses, collection, funding and recovery into an editable handover. Channel commitments remain distinct from on-chain transfers and from our historical purchase count. |
| ApiCharge | Its documented route grants retain purchased service levels, explicit limits and pricing terms, supporting paid access to a merchant's service. | Carry the selected access terms into the customer's complete source/query, output and later work, while retaining the original purchased allowance and consumption when defaults change. |
| MPP Router | Its documented Stellar-funded proxy provides paid access, request-ID response caching and authenticated asynchronous job polling. | Keep the reviewed request contents, source and payment selection bound to the original operation across interrupted delivery, complete output collection and independently received editable workspaces. |

The table places each provider's documented payment or access capability alongside our implemented customer workflow. The technical contribution is the retained relationship across the whole task. Its business value is continuity for the customer and the next operator: they can inspect what was approved and paid, distinguish a pending payment from incomplete delivery, and continue the original work after an interruption or handover.

The completed team-operated evidence includes a 144-row HPD task delivered in two pages of 100 and 44 rows, with two distinct 10,000-stroop payments. A separate original MySQL purchase returned 100 rows and 14 columns with its actual truncation flag; its complete response and purchase history reopened in another directory with signing disabled. These establish working pagination, paid-result retention and receiving in those particular team-operated testnet cases. The historical baseline remains 18 distinct team-operated testnet purchases totaling 411,000 stroops, with fees separate. Source checks, analytical runs and Channel commitments retain their separate classifications.

The six funded packages turn that foundation into two independently operated merchant installations and two buyers. Each first-tranche installation must complete an approved native Stellar testnet purchase and useful output. The two-facilitator comparison then holds the useful service, schema, source population and output constant; the retained Vellar/Periplo catalogue comparison found no shared seller/route identity, so registration and integration of that same service remain funded work. Later acceptance measures the already-defined recurring-use cohorts and both merchants' mainnet operation. Reusable source/consumer, deployment and receiving improvements return to the public product.
